# Controlled inspection-value diagnostic



This grid uses one predeclared validation snapshot after 90 calendar minutes, with a wider initial health prior. It is not held-out policy-performance evidence.

Columns compare the estimated incremental cost of inspection with processing or maintenance. Negative values favour inspection under the stated model and horizon.

24 matched latent worlds per action and a 180-minute calendar horizon are used. Intervals describe Monte Carlo sampling error; they do not cover model error.

Future arrivals are forecasts conditional on the public daily count/history. Future job attributes use training benchmark-duration pools, so held-out source-group mismatch is an explicit forecasting limitation.

No residual-value approximation is added at the diagnostic horizon. A deferred breakdown beyond that horizon is not counted. Full policy experiments are separate.

Inspection had lower mean estimated cost than both alternatives in 0/12 grid cells.

Both paired upper interval bounds were below zero in 0/12 cells.

If changing peak headroom barely changes these values, this diagnostic does not support a strong billing-history interaction. Do not hide that outcome.

The value-of-information concept is established research. This diagnostic investigates a particular scheduling interaction, not a new general theorem.
